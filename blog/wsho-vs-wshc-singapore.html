WSH Officer or WSH Coordinator? They are not the same.

Qualifications, job scopes, legal requirements, and salary differences between Singapore’s two most confused safety roles — and why getting it wrong exposes you to MOM fines.

By OSS → 6 min read → WSH Roles

For many business owners, project managers, and HR professionals in Singapore’s construction, marine, and manufacturing sectors, two job titles appear constantly in regulations, job postings, and safety plans: WSH Officer and WSH Coordinator.

Are they interchangeable? Can a Coordinator do the job of an Officer? Do both require MOM registration? Getting this wrong has direct legal and financial consequences — MOM fines, Stop-Work Orders, and director liability under Section 48(1) of the WSH Act.

A WSH Coordinator cannot perform the statutory duties of a registered WSH Officer. Appointing one when you legally need the other is a breach of the WSH Act.

1. The fundamental difference — qualification and registration

The most important distinction lies in their training pathway and regulatory status under the WSH Professionals WSQ Framework, developed jointly by MOM and SkillsFuture Singapore.1

Level B — Coordinator

Qualification: WSQ Advanced Certificate in WSH (ACWSH)

MOM Registration: Not required3

Salary: S$2,500 – S$4,500/month4

Reports to: Safety Manager or WSH Officer

Level C — Officer

Qualification: WSQ Specialist Diploma in WSH (SDWSH)

MOM Registration: Mandatory — a formal legal status6

Salary: S$6,000 – S$7,000/month7

Reports to: Project Manager or Top Management

2. Career progression pathway

WSH Professionals WSQ Framework8
Level Designation Qualification MOM Registration
Level A Certified WSH Professional Entry-level certification No
Level B WSH Coordinator Advanced Certificate (ACWSH) No
Level C WSH Officer Specialist Diploma (SDWSH) Yes — Mandatory
Level D WSH Auditor Graduate Certificate Yes

A Coordinator typically gains 2–3 years of experience before pursuing the Specialist Diploma. The salary jump from Level B to Level C reflects the increased legal accountability.

4. When is each role legally required?

Legal appointment requirements
Project / Workplace Required Role Legal Basis
Construction — contract sum ≥ S$10 million WSH Officer WSH (Construction) Regs 2007, Reg. 7(1)12
Shipyards (any size) WSH Officer WSH (Shipbuilding & Ship-Repair) Regs 2008, Reg. 6(1)13
Factories — 100+ workers WSH Officer WSH (General Provisions) Regs, Reg. 3(1)14
Construction — contract sum < S$10 million WSH Coordinator WSH (Construction) Regs 2007, Reg. 815
Low-risk projects WSHC may suffice Based on risk assessment under WSH (Risk Management) Regs

Note that companies with high-risk work (demolition, work at height, confined spaces) but a contract sum below S$10 million may only be legally required to appoint a Coordinator — but a part-time WSH Officer is often the smarter choice. A part-time WSHO provides the statutory authority of a full Officer at a fraction of the cost.16

5. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming the titles are interchangeable — a Coordinator cannot exercise the statutory duties of a registered WSHO. This is a breach of the WSH (Construction) Regulations, subject to fines and Stop-Work Orders.
  • Giving an employee the title without MOM registration — only MOM-registered individuals can legally call themselves WSH Officers. A Specialist Diploma without registration is not sufficient.
  • Underestimating the salary difference — offering Coordinator pay (S$2,500–S$4,500) to recruit a registered WSHO will fail. Budget S$6,000–S$7,000 for a full-time Officer.
  • Not reviewing appointments when project scope changes — a project that crosses the S$10 million threshold via variation orders must replace its Coordinator with a registered WSHO. This is a common compliance gap.

6. The bottom line

The difference between a WSH Officer and a WSH Coordinator in Singapore is legally defined — not just a matter of job title or seniority. A Coordinator (Level B) is sufficient for smaller construction projects under S$10 million. An Officer (Level C) is mandatory for large construction sites, shipyards, and large factories, and carries full statutory legal liability.

For business owners: appoint the correct role for your project’s size and risk profile. Misclassification exposes your company to MOM penalties, Stop-Work Orders, and director liability under Section 48(1) of the WSH Act.
