    Frequently Asked Questions

    Plain answers to real questions

    Everything SME owners ask before signing up — answered honestly, without the jargon.

    General
    Do I actually need a WSH officer?+
    It depends on your industry and headcount. Under the WSH Act, certain higher-risk industries — including construction, marine, and some manufacturing sectors — are legally required to appoint a registered WSH Officer once they hit certain headcount thresholds. For most F&B, retail, and office businesses, it’s not a legal requirement — but you are still legally responsible for workplace safety. That means someone in your business must own it. OSS acts as your fractional safety team, so you get professional-grade coverage without needing to hire in-house.
    We’re a small business. Is this really for us?+
    Yes — that’s exactly who OSS is built for. Large companies have in-house safety teams. SMEs don’t, but they face the same MOM requirements and the same enforcement risk. In fact, small businesses are disproportionately affected by safety incidents because they have fewer resources to absorb a fine, a stop-work order, or a worker injury claim. OSS makes professional WSH support accessible and affordable for businesses with 5 to 300 staff.
    What happens if MOM visits and we’re not compliant?+
    MOM inspectors can issue Improvement Notices (requiring you to fix something within a set timeframe), Prohibition Notices (stopping specific activities immediately), or Stop-Work Orders (shutting down your entire operation). Fines for WSH Act breaches can reach S$500,000 for corporations, and repeated offences carry heavier penalties. Beyond the fine, the reputational damage and lost revenue from a closure are often far more damaging than the fine itself.
    Is OSS the same as having an in-house safety officer?+
    Similar, but more cost-effective. An in-house WSH Officer in Singapore costs S$4,000–S$7,000/month in salary alone — before CPF, benefits, and training. OSS delivers the same core function at a fraction of the cost, structured as a subscription. The key difference is that an in-house hire is on-site full time; OSS works on a scheduled visit and on-call basis. For most SMEs, that coverage level is exactly what’s needed.
    Pricing & Subscription
    Why is WSH support a subscription? Can’t I just do it once?+
    Workplace safety is not a one-time project — it’s an ongoing obligation. Risk assessments need to be reviewed when processes change. New workers need to be briefed. Incidents need to be documented. Documents need to stay current. A one-time setup gives you a false sense of security; six months later your documents are outdated and you’re exposed again. The subscription model ensures your safety system is always maintained, not just installed.
    What’s included in each package?+
    All packages include risk assessments, safe work procedures, monthly or quarterly site inspections, incident reporting support, and WhatsApp access to your WSHO. Higher tiers add more frequent visits, more documents covered, toolbox talks, and for the top tier, formal appointment as your named WSHO under the WSH Act. See the full breakdown on the Packages page.

    Are there any setup fees or hidden costs?+
    No setup fees. The monthly subscription covers everything listed in your package. The only additional cost would be if you need specialist work outside the scope of your plan — for example, a formal COSHH assessment for a complex chemical environment, or a full-day training session. We’ll always quote these separately and never bill you for them without your sign-off.
    What package should I start with?+
    OSS Watch (S$650/month) is right for micro-businesses under 20 staff with lower operational risk — retail, office, light F&B. OSS Guard (S$1,200/month) suits businesses with 15–80 staff in higher-risk settings like kitchens, workshops, and warehouses. OSS Overwatch (S$2,500/month) is for businesses that legally need a named WSHO, or that operate in high-risk environments with 50+ staff. If you’re unsure, book a free 30-minute call and we’ll tell you exactly which tier makes sense.
    MOM & Compliance
    What is the WSH Act and does it apply to me?+
    The Workplace Safety and Health Act is Singapore’s primary legislation governing occupational safety. It applies to virtually all workplaces in Singapore, regardless of size or industry. Under the Act, employers have a legal duty to take reasonably practicable measures to ensure the safety and health of all workers. This includes having risk assessments, safe work procedures, trained workers, and a system for managing incidents.
    What is bizSAFE and do I need it?+
    bizSAFE is a workplace safety certification programme run by WSH Council. It has five levels (1–Star). bizSAFE Level 3 is required by many government agencies and large contractors as a procurement condition, meaning you can’t tender for their work without it. If you supply to hospitals, public agencies, large F&B chains, or construction main contractors, you likely need it. OSS can guide you through the certification process. Read our full guide here.
    What incidents must I report to MOM?+
    You must report to MOM within 10 days: fatalities, injuries requiring hospitalisation, injuries resulting in more than 3 consecutive days of medical leave, and dangerous occurrences (such as structural collapses or chemical releases) regardless of injury. Failure to report is a criminal offence under the WSH Act, with fines up to S$5,000 for a first offence. OSS clients are supported through the reporting process whenever an incident occurs.
    We’ve already had a MOM notice. Can OSS still help?+
    Yes, and this is one of the most common situations we step into. We can help you respond to the notice, implement the required corrective actions, and build a documented system that demonstrates ongoing compliance to MOM. Acting quickly and systematically after a notice significantly reduces the risk of escalation. Book a call and bring the notice with you — we’ll tell you exactly what’s needed.
    What OSS Does
    What exactly happens during a site inspection?+
    Your WSHO visits your premises and walks through all work areas, checking for hazards, verifying that controls are in place, and confirming that your documentation reflects what’s actually happening on the ground. After each visit, you receive a written inspection report with findings graded by risk level, corrective actions required, and a tracker to manage close-out. This report is your evidence of due diligence if MOM ever asks.
    Who does OSS cover — just our full-time staff?+
    No. WSH obligations extend to everyone on your premises — full-time staff, part-timers, contractors, delivery personnel, and visitors. Your risk assessments and safe work procedures must account for all work activities, regardless of who performs them. OSS helps ensure your documentation covers the actual exposure, not just what’s on your payroll.
    How quickly can OSS respond if something happens?+
    All OSS clients have direct WhatsApp access to their WSHO. For urgent situations — an incident, an MOM visit, an injury — we respond within the hour during business hours. For after-hours emergencies, we do our best to respond as quickly as possible. We help you manage the immediate situation, document correctly, and notify MOM if required.
    Do we own our safety documents?+
    Yes, completely. Every document OSS creates for you belongs to you. If you ever leave, all your risk assessments, SWPs, inspection reports, and training records are handed over in full. We don’t hold your documents hostage, and we don’t use generic templates — everything is specific to your business.
    Getting Started
    How does onboarding work?+
    After you sign up, we schedule an initial site visit within 5 working days. Your WSHO walks through your premises, understands your operations, and identifies your key risks. From that visit, we build your WSH baseline — your initial risk assessments, safe work procedures, and a compliance gap report. Most clients have their core documents in place within 3–4 weeks of starting.
    What do I need to prepare before we start?+
    Nothing formal. We’ll ask you to walk us through your operations during the first visit — what tasks your workers do, what equipment is used, what chemicals or substances are on site, and what incidents or near misses have happened before. You don’t need to have any existing safety documents — we start from wherever you are.
    How long until we’re fully compliant?+
    For a typical SME with no existing safety documentation, a solid baseline is achievable in 4–8 weeks. This covers your WSH policy, risk assessments, safe work procedures, and initial worker briefing records. Full compliance — including training completion, drill records, and a reviewed corrective action tracker — usually takes 2–3 months. We work at your pace and prioritise the highest-risk gaps first.

You can also take our free WSH self-audit right now to see how your workplace compares against MOM requirements — it takes 2 minutes and gives you an instant result.
